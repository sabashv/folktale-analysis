## Problem: 
The concept of a motif in folklore studies has long been theoretically unstable. In Stith Thompson’s classical formulation, a motif is defined as the “smallest element in a tale having the power to persist in tradition,” often catalogued as concrete narrative incidents, characters, or situations. Thompson’s Motif-Index of Folk Literature operationalizes this idea by enumerating thousands of such recurring elements. However, many scholars have criticized this definition for being inconsistent and overly eclectic: the units indexed by Thompson range from highly concrete actions (“War between domestic and wild animals”) to abstract thematic labels (“Animals with human traits”), making the boundary of what counts as a motif unclear. The lack of a principled structural criterion has led to ongoing debate about whether motifs are narrative functions, symbolic themes, or simply recurring textual fragments. In contrast, Harris’ distributional theory of sublanguage offers a more formal perspective: instead of defining motifs semantically in advance, recurring narrative units can be identified empirically through their distributional stability across texts. From this viewpoint, motifs emerge as statistically regular, reusable structures within a narrative sublanguage [^1]

## Two Extraction Approaches:
1. Unit-Based Extraction (Harris-Oriented): Instead of defining motifs theoretically, we attempt to induce them empirically from distributional behavior.
2. Motif-Based Extraction (more Thompson oriented): We directly prompt the LLM to extract motifs.


### Approach 1: Unit-Based Extraction
###### Step1: LLM extracts narrative units:
Prompted to produce:
* Minimal, concrete narrative actions
* No abstract concepts
* No compound events
* 5–15 most important units

##### Step2: Step 2: Normalization
* To reduce story-specific noise: Proper names replaced with placeholders (PERSON)

##### Step 3: Clustering

* All extracted units are embedded and clustered.
* Important constraint: Clusters containing units from only one story are removed. A cluster is a motif only if it appears across multiple stories.


### Approach 2: Motif-Based Extraction

##### Step1: LLM extracts narrative units:
Prompted to extract motifs as defined by Thompson:
* motif is the smallest element in a tale having a power to persist in tradition. In order to have this power it must have something unusual and striking about it.

##### Step2: Deduplication:
* To normalize synonym motifs we use cosine similarity with thereshold 0.75 (I think we should run the experiments again without this step)

##### Step 3: Clustering
* All extracted units are embedded and clustered. All clusters treated as motifs.
